## TED: calendar facts are not revision timestamps

The premise of #22 is correct: `2026-07-02+02:00` names the Official Journal's
2 July publication day. Representing it as a midnight instant and displaying it
in London gives 1 July. However, the proposed model migration also changed
`updated_at` and `last_material_update`, and guessed the precision of stored
midnight timestamps.

An independent counterexample uses a TED release at `2026-07-01T22:00:00Z` and
a linked German cancellation at `2026-07-01T23:30:00Z`. Promoting the TED revision
to `2026-07-02` makes the cancellation look older and prevents its application.
The migration also rewrote an explicit clock timestamp and a national-source
revision attached to TED lineage. Matching reviewed hashes does not establish
that revision ordering remains correct.

The repair stores only new TED `published_at` and its publication provenance as
the explicit calendar day. `updated_at` and `last_material_update` keep the
existing instant, and real clock-bearing input retains its precision. No retained
row migration, revision reordering or review-hash exception is introduced.

Existing TED publication instants display in the publisher's Brussels calendar.
An explicitly matching non-TED provenance date retains national-source handling.
Award dates, deadlines, collection times and material-update times continue using
their existing formatters. The redesigned detail view does not label a change in
date representation as a source update.

## Descriptions: presentation cleanup without evidence churn

#23 and #30 identify real distracting prose. Their original implementations
change canonical descriptions when sources are collected again. That causes
material-update badges, new translation keys and stale review hashes. In #30's
own audit, three reviewed recommendations would disappear until re-reviewed.
The #23 audit also found affected reviewed exclusions; silently losing exclusions
can bring previously rejected records back into the feed.

The replacement operates only in `useSignalText`:

- Digital Outcomes removes the exact GOV.UK banner and breadcrumb prefix, plus
  the exact terminal GCA/CCS help block. Buyer search criteria and technology
  requirements remain. Look-alike buyer text and unfamiliar page structures remain.
- OCDS suffix cleanup requires every appended lot to match the structured source
  IDs, titles and descriptions. Only empty/null artefacts are tidied; substantive
  text is preserved. Truncated, translated or mismatched suffixes remain intact.
- English and original views both reuse the existing text. Canonical descriptions,
  cached translations, evidence quotes, source links and search indexes are unchanged.
  Lot IDs remain in the structured facts and in the original searchable text.
- A notice consisting solely of an empty lot suffix is retained rather than
  turned into an empty description.

No broad keyword removal, automatic re-review, extra API request, data rewrite,
test bypass or larger collection budget is part of these repairs.
