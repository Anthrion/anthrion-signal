# Relevance, collection and awarded-record audit — 18 September 2026

Signal now separates open opportunities from confirmed historical awards, broadens explicit digital-delivery discovery, and removes misleading capability matches caused by bidding instructions or unrelated human/physical services. The policy preserves uncertain and mixed procurements for review instead of rejecting everything without a familiar keyword.

## What was audited

- Frozen comparison time: **18 September 2026, 06:43:47 UTC**. Baseline source/data revision: `c199809567c9895c25f497564fdd1586e610072f`.
- **65,169 retained notice identities**, from **76,712 stored versions** across canonical records, archives and replayable rejections. The reconciled canonical record takes precedence over older versions. A notice identity is not a unique contract: separate lots, notices, amendments and sources can describe the same procurement.
- **2,857 published opportunity-feed records** at the baseline. Historical awards were previously absent from the public feed.
- Every retained notice was replayed through the old and revised rules at the same observation time. Source text, hashes and provenance were preserved. Relevance and current availability were measured separately.
- Manual review covered every proposed removal from the public feed, newly recovered candidate scopes, boundary cases and stratified examples. This is an automated census plus targeted human-style review; it is not a claim that all 65,169 full procurement documents were read or that precision/recall is 100%.
- Regression evidence includes **195 real notice fixtures**, plus synthetic counterexamples designed to protect unfamiliar buyers, mixed lots, non-English scope, negation and missing classifications. These are regression controls, not an independent statistical accuracy estimate.

The machine-readable [summary](audits/2026-09-18/summary.json), [data profile](audits/2026-09-18/profile.json), [changed decisions](audits/2026-09-18/changed-decisions.csv) and [companion notebook](audits/2026-09-18/relevance-audit.ipynb) preserve the counts and calculations. The full local decision register is generated by `scripts/compare_relevance_audit.py`.

## Findings and changes

| Finding | Evidence and impact | Change | Severity / confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supplier portals were mistaken for purchased portals | Bidding instructions added portal/Salesforce relevance to physical and human services. | Administrative sentences require both bidding context and submission/registration activity before being ignored; an actual portal build remains eligible. | Medium / high for reviewed removals |
| Generic management and integration language was overbroad | Human complaint-handling training, occupational coding and mechanical integration acquired software tags. | Require local software evidence for ambiguous business language; distinguish mechanical connections and operational data entry from application delivery. | Medium / high for reviewed cases |
| Clear digital work could be missed without an expected CPV or product name | DfE multidisciplinary digital delivery and Scotland's radiology learning platform were below the old threshold. | Shared collection/matching vocabulary covers explicit DDaT delivery, digital service lifecycle, online learning, incident/patient systems and related needs. Generic “digital solutions” alone is insufficient. | High / high for recovered cases |
| Digital Outcomes deadlines were misread or absent | Numeric UK dates and date-first timelines were missed; later invitation-only stages could obscure a closed first stage. | Source-specific parsing supports UK numeric and named dates, explicit times and Europe/London DST. Initial submission takes precedence. Date-only deadlines stay date-only and remain available throughout their UK calendar day. | High / high for reviewed timelines |
| Old planned start dates were treated too confidently | Nine previously published Digital Outcomes notices have a past planned start but no reliably dated current application deadline. | Keep them in **All Signals** as **Application window unconfirmed**, while excluding them from **Live Opportunities**. Do not invent a deadline/year or assert closure from a planned start. | Medium / high about uncertainty |
| Historical awards were inaccessible | Relevant retained awards were outside the active-opportunity export. | Separate, lazy-loaded market files expose confirmed awards; rejected and archived records are reclassified under the current policy. | Medium / high |
| Some collectors have incomplete coverage | Wales upstream conversion errors and outstanding GOV.UK, TED and Spain backfill/query windows were present in the baseline. | Preserve resumable checkpoints and partial-result safeguards; broaden discovery without resetting all history or increasing paid-service use. | Medium / known limitation |

## Using Awarded

Open the sort menu and select **Awarded**, immediately below **Capability A–Z**. The heading becomes **Awarded contracts**. Selecting another sort returns to **All Signals**.

- Salesforce/CRM and related platform capabilities appear first, standalone AI/agent work next, and other retained digital work after that. Each group uses the most recent notice update/publication date first, with stable ID tie-breaking.
- Search, market, capability, buyer, value and source filters remain available. Deadline and opportunity-freshness controls are hidden because they would misleadingly filter historical awards.
- Records show the published supplier, where available, and **Award notice** date. This date is the notice's update/publication date, not an invented contract-signing or award-decision timestamp. Contract dates and original evidence remain in the detailed record.
- Cancelled, withdrawn, unsuccessful and pending-only award records, intended direct-award transparency notices, and inferred renewals are excluded. A current cancellation supersedes an older archived award. Known OCDS supplier attribution uses active awards.
- Awards stay out of live/pipeline counts. They can be saved, hidden, searched and shared, and award deep links survive reload. Historical notices do not offer an expired-deadline calendar action.
- The selected market's history downloads only when needed; normal browsing does not fetch the archive. Download/validation failures show a retry action instead of a misleading empty result. Existing English translations are reused; missing translations retain the original source language and do not create new model requests for the archive.

“All awards” means all relevant confirmed awards in the retained source corpus for the selected market. It does not mean every government contract ever awarded or a deduplicated list of unique contracts.

The initial export contains **16,048 unique notice identities**, represented by **16,068 market entries** because some notices cover multiple markets: UK 7,526; Germany 2,767; Spain 3,205; Nordics 1,643; Italy 456; Greece 373; US 98.

## Coverage and data-quality limits

At the frozen baseline, **8 of 13 enabled sources were healthy**, four were partial and LA RAMP failed its completeness check. Counts in source health represent the latest collection batch, not total lifetime coverage.

| Source | Observed limitation | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sell2Wales | HTTP 500 converting source data; ten listing records retained. | Partial coverage remains explicit; do not infer a complete empty result. |
| GOV.UK | Some search queries are catching up. | Retain offsets and continue bounded query rotation. |
| TED | Additional keyword windows are catching up; last complete success recorded 13 September. | Preserve classification results, keyword backfill and page budgets. |
| Spain PLACSP | Historical window exceeded the per-run page budget. | Resume older pages in subsequent runs. |
| LA RAMP | Earlier incomplete open-opportunity snapshot. | A read-only recheck at **09:43:11 UTC** returned **431 normalised records** and healthy completeness. The existing guard correctly prevented deletion from the incomplete response. |

The wider profile has no missing titles/source URLs, invalid observation timestamps or negative maximum values. It has **521 missing descriptions (0.80%)**, **11,009 missing CPV classifications (16.89%)** and substantial expected missing deadlines/budgets in historical, planning and publication records. Missing fields are not evidence of irrelevance.

There are **57 retained records with a minimum value greater than the maximum**, none in the baseline public opportunity feed. The audit records these as unresolved source/normalisation inconsistencies; it does not swap amounts or invent corrected budgets. The current UI displays and filters the published maximum value. Supplier and value completeness must be considered before using history for market-size or win-rate analysis.

This release does not enable additional sources, change company GitHub settings or buy external services. Search recall outside the collected corpus cannot be measured from retained data alone; pending backfills and sparse untranslated notices remain limits. A guaranteed “no missed opportunities” claim would be unsupported.

## Verification and reproducibility

The release checks cover source-faithful classification, replay of prior rejections, mixed-scope counterexamples, UK dates and first-stage deadlines, archived cancellations, failed awards, market partitioning, cache invalidation, publication on award-only changes, desktop/mobile navigation, keyboard accessibility, saved/share links and retry behaviour.

Local verification: **745 pipeline tests**, **40 frontend unit tests**, production build and public-output validation passed. The full browser run passed 117 checks with one intentional screenshot-only skip; the two market-count checks initially crossed real deadlines and passed after fixing their snapshot clock. Real-time expiry still has separate regression coverage. All **14 final desktop/mobile award and data-refresh checks** passed against the rebuilt files.
